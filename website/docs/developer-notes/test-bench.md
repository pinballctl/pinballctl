## Single power inlet — proposed revision

**Latest requirement: one external low-voltage power source, with a neat inlet on the right side.** The revision 5 drawings and fuse schedule below describe the earlier two-adapter arrangement. The shopping list now selects the Yahboom board as P24, but its mounted position and the replacement supply/inlet are not yet included in those drawings. Do not treat their power-entry holes, fuse values or Pi stack clearance as approved for this change.

The recommended arrangement is an enclosed external **12 V supply → right-side XT60 inlet → main fuse → separately fused branches** for the Pi converter, the existing lighting/logic converter and the 12 V indicator lamps/button rings. The Pi converter powers Pi USB-C for portable demos. The ESP normally connects to the Mac over USB; its lead is manually swapped to the Pi for demos. Only the grounds are common: do not parallel the converters' 5 V outputs or join the lighting rail to USB VBUS. Mains stays outside the wooden box. Mac/Pi host changes use a manually swapped USB data cable inside the box; no USB switch is required.

### Supply sizing

The following is a planning calculation, not measured consumption. It covers the present lamp-output bench, not future real pinball coils.

| Load allowance | Power |
| --- | ---: |
| Pi and its USB-powered ESP/peripherals, within the Pi supply budget | 25.5 W |
| Up to 102 pixels, conservatively allowing 60 mA each at 5 V | 30.6 W |
| Sensors, LCD and interface reserve at 5 V | 5 W |
| Two 12 V lamp indicators and ten button rings, provisional reserve | 10 W |

At an assumed 90% conversion efficiency, **(25.5 + 30.6 + 5) / 0.90 + 10 = 77.9 W**, or **6.5 A from 12 V**. At 80%, this becomes **86.4 W / 7.2 A**. A reputable enclosed **12 V / 10 A (120 W)** supply is therefore a reasonable target with headroom; the linked 20 A unit has extra capacity but is not the only workable current rating. No alternative 10 A Amazon product has yet been selected. Add power in watts across different voltages, not the 5 V output amps directly to the 12 V input amps.

Before promoting this proposal into the build schedule, allocate the Pi converter and connector in the measured layout, choose main/branch fuse values against the actual wire and connector ratings, and verify voltage drop, Pi supply detection and temperatures under the intended lighting load. The existing 5 A input-fuse schedule applies only to the earlier separate lighting supply. The new Pi branch must not simply be added behind it.

### Normal Mac use and portable Pi demos

**The Mac is the normal development/testing host. The Pi is the portable demo host. No USB switch, hub or selector is added to the box or shopping list.** Keep the ESP's serial/programming USB-C port reachable when the console panel is lifted.

- **Normal use:** connect the Mac's USB data cable directly to that ESP port. Run the bench's host software on the Mac; the Pi is not part of this USB connection.
- **Portable demo:** disconnect the Mac cable at the ESP and connect P23 from Pi USB-A to the **same ESP port**. The internal Yahboom P24 board powers the Pi from the bench's single 12 V inlet. The Pi then runs the host software without the Mac.
- **Changing hosts:** disable outputs and stop the current host's bridge/serial/flashing application before unplugging. Swap the lead, then start the bridge on the newly connected host. Keep both hosts' firmware/configuration requirements consistent; swapping the cable does not copy configuration or media between them.

The USB lead carries data and the ESP's bus power from the connected host. Expect a disconnect/reconnect and possible ESP restart when swapping; do not rely on continuous operation. Do not connect both hosts through the ESP's different USB sockets, add a Y cable, or add a separate 5 V feed to the USB-powered ESP rail. The single external 12 V supply powers the other bench loads in either mode; it also supplies the Pi through P24 when the Pi is used. No additional right-side USB connector is required for this manual-access arrangement. The Mac's existing data cable can be reused; a new Mac cable is not included in the price total.

## How the ball course works

1. Three 27 mm balls queue in the low front-left trough. T1 is nearest the front end stop, then T2 and T3.
2. Lift T1's ball **by hand** into the higher rear-right shooter pocket. The other two balls roll forward and change the trough signals naturally. Once settled, T1 and T2 are occupied and T3 is empty.
3. The ball rests against the manual gate and blocks **Ready**. The **Fire** button is a separate input. It does not move a solenoid or open the gate.
4. Lift the gate 30 mm. The ball clears Ready, crosses Onfield, Bumper 1, the corner, Bumper 2 and Drain, then rejoins the trough at T3.
5. All three trough positions are occupied again. Repeat the hand transfer for the next launch.

Bumper stations detect passage; they do not kick the ball. This tests real ball occupancy and moving-ball events. It does not reproduce the forces of a complete pinball playfield or an automatic trough ejector. Hand transfer is the agreed first-build method.

Eight sensor pairs means **16 physical heads**. Mount each on an adjustable plywood pad outside the rails, with the optical axis initially **24 mm above the local running surface**. Start the pads with laminated 12 + 6 mm offcuts and shim to the measured lens centre; do not assume the lens is exactly halfway up its housing. Drill aligned beam apertures through both rails, starting around 3 mm and enlarging only if alignment requires it. Fix the heads through their flanges with small screws and nylon washers, without distorting the housing.

For a 27 mm ball, radius r=13.5 and beam height h=24, the ideal blocked length is `2 × √(13.5² − (24 − 13.5)²) = 16.97 mm`. Touching balls therefore leave an ideal **27 − 16.97 = 10.03 mm** clear window. This is more generous than the previous 4.55 mm window at h=21. Finite beam width, reflections, ball speed and debounce shorten the real clear interval. At an illustrative 500 mm/s, 10 mm lasts only 20 ms even before those losses: test the actual event stream rather than assuming a debounce setting will preserve every transition.

The blue rings are fixed-colour 12–24 V indicators, not WS2812 pixels. They add no GPIOs in this steady-backlight arrangement. The [button mounting and terminal drawing](./media/test-bench-parts/p06-dimensions.jpg) is from the selected Amazon listing. Solder four insulated leads per button and branch the ring-light bus in 22 AWG, using retained soldered joints and the listed shrink. The schedule uses 11 of the supplied 10 kΩ resistors, ten 4.7 kΩ resistors and one 330 Ω resistor. It requires soldering a small interface circuit; it is not a fully plug-together kit. Route the LED power bus in 18 AWG wire and WAGO connectors, not through thin perfboard traces or the ESP breakout.

**New power revision:** P24 Yahboom mounting height, cooler clearance, bridge alignment and terminal access are not included in the revision 5 collision checks. The replacement XT60 inlet needs verified flange/cutout dimensions, wall attachment and strain relief. Main/branch fuse positions and wire sizing need updating for the combined load.

**Unresolved dimensions, explicitly visible:** P06 button overall depth/cap height and wired clearance; P07 lamp body/cap/grip; P08 LCD complete thickness/bezel/hole centres; P13/P14 populated ESP height and cable mouldings; P09/F09 corner bend radius, adhesive retention and clip engagement; F12 catch pattern/projection; F13 stay pivot geometry and conflicting 190/195 mm length. Socket P27 body/grip is also unpublished, which is why it is used as a restrained pigtail rather than a panel-mounted fitting. These prevent a blanket “every dimension verified” claim. Packaging measurements, an AI illustration, and a nominal collision check are not substitutes.

Before making the final console holes, place the actual buttons, lamps, LCD and controller assemblies against the reserved envelopes. If a lamp exceeds either allowance, the current closed-height layout fails and must change. Do not silently shorten its wiring allowance or remove the lid clearance to make a drawing pass.

Then test one optical pair with touching balls at the chosen height, all eight stations through repeated cycles, the gate, the continuous-fence corner and the transport cover. Test power-up/reset OFF, input polarity, LED full-white supply voltage, cable temperature and Pi/converter temperature with the intended ventilation. Physical build, closure, transport and electrical load tests have **not** been performed by this documentation work.

The large lamps demonstrate output commands and light-load switching. They do not validate real solenoid current, flyback, pulse timing under a coil load or flipper mechanics. No application, bridge, firmware or runtime GPIO changes are included. Existing arming, watchdog, pulse-limit and fault behaviour must remain enforced.
